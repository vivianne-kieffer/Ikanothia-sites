Speak to Lead: Why Coaching and Public Speaking Propel Women's Careers After 35

How to make your voice — and your value — truly seen and heard.

Bloom After 35

For many professional women, turning 35 marks not a slowdown, but a crossroads. It's a stage rich with expertise, credibility, and experience — yet often shadowed by an invisible ceiling.

The question becomes less about ability and more about visibility: How can I make my voice — and my value — truly seen and heard?

The answer, increasingly, lies in the intersection of coaching and communication. Coaching sharpens clarity and direction. Public speaking amplifies presence and influence. Together, they redefine what leadership looks like for women in mid-career and beyond.

The Mid-Career Crossroads

By their mid-thirties, most women have proven themselves many times over. Yet, despite achievement, advancement often stalls. According to McKinsey's Women in the Workplace 2024, women make up nearly 48% of entry-level roles but hold only 28% of senior vice-president positions. The "broken rung" — that first missed step on the leadership ladder — compounds over time, keeping women from executive tracks.

At this stage, many women seek reinvention — not just a promotion, but a sense of alignment. As Case Western Reserve University's research on women's career phases shows, this is when professionals often shift from achievement to authentic contribution: leading in a way that feels both powerful and personal.

Coaching: Clarity and Confidence in Action

Coaching offers a rare space to pause, reflect, and plan strategically. It turns professional experience into a clear leadership path, pairing structure with self-awareness. The International Coaching Federation (ICF) reports that 85% of clients experience greater self-confidence, and over 70% improve work performance, communication, and relationships through coaching. Organizations that invest in coaching see returns of up to 7 times their original investment.

For women over 35, coaching becomes more than skill-building — it's identity-building. It helps clarify "what's next," reconnects values with goals, and transforms quiet capability into visible leadership.

Public Speaking: The Career Multiplier

Visibility creates velocity — and presentation skills drive both. According to the Market.biz 2024 Public Speaking Statistics Report, 59% of hiring managers consider public speaking and presentation abilities an important factor in evaluating candidates. Yet fear of public speaking — known as glossophobia — can quietly limit potential, reducing wages by up to 10% and career advancement opportunities by roughly 15%.

These aren't just numbers; they reflect lost opportunities for visibility, recognition, and influence. In workplaces where leadership often means being heard, presentation skills are not a "nice to have" — they're career infrastructure.

A Forbes/Prezi survey found that 70% of professionals who regularly give presentations say these skills are critical to their success at work. And communication studies suggest that strong presentation skills have an even greater career impact for women than for men, since women's communication style and visibility are often more tightly linked to perceptions of leadership potential.

In other words, the ability to speak with authority, authenticity, and ease doesn't just enhance confidence — it changes how others perceive your readiness for leadership.

From Acceleration to Alignment

At Bloom after 35, we see a pattern again and again: women don't need to prove more — they need to be seen differently. Coaching builds the clarity and mindset; public speaking magnifies it. Together, they help women move from quiet competence to visible confidence.

The result is not just career acceleration, but alignment — a stage where leadership feels grounded, purposeful, and authentically your own.

A Call to Lead Your Next Chapter

If you're a woman over 35, you already have the experience. What's next is making your voice the vessel of your leadership.

Because when women speak with presence, they don't just change their careers — they change the conversation.
