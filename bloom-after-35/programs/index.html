The work of
beginning again

An eight-session coaching program for women in transition — and a way to pass it on to the woman coming behind you.

Vivianne Kieffer · Founder & Coach · ICF ACC

Who this is for

Women in the middle of a change

Women mid-crossing — where the old ground has gone and the new ground has not yet arrived. You are not behind. You are between.

Changing careers

You are excellent at something you no longer want to do

The competence is real and so is the exhaustion. The difficulty is not ability — it is that leaving something you are good at looks, from the outside, like throwing it away.

Changing countries

Your credentials crossed the border. Your standing did not

A new market reads your history in a language it does not speak. You are starting from a rung well below the one you left, with all of the judgement and none of the recognition.

Restarting a career

You did not stop being capable. You stopped being visible

After caregiving, illness, a move or a redundancy, the hardest part is rarely the skill. It is the gap on the page, and the story you have been taught to tell about it.

Motherhood and work

Two whole identities, one calendar

Both are real, both are yours, and neither is a hobby. The work is holding them at the same time without disappearing into either one.

The program

Eight sessions, one crossing

Eight one-hour sessions across six to eight months, by video call, in English or Spanish. The spacing is deliberate — real change needs weeks between sessions in which to happen. The arc builds the ground before we choose a direction, and tests the direction before you commit to it.

Format

8 × 60 minutes

Every two to four weeks, scheduled to your life rather than a fixed cohort. Rescheduling is expected, not penalised.

Between sessions

The unthinkable, in steps

What happens between sessions is yours to choose and yours to carry — I cannot promise the doing. What I can promise is that we break what looks impossible into moves small enough to be taken. Short written support between sessions is included.

Standard

ICF-aligned coaching

Delivered to International Coaching Federation core competencies and code of ethics. What is said in the session stays in the session.

Why months, not weeks

The change happens between the sessions, not inside them

A session is where you see something clearly. The weeks that follow are where the seeing turns into a conversation you actually have, an application you actually send, a boundary you actually hold. Compress the sessions and you get insight without evidence — which feels wonderful for a fortnight and changes nothing.

A transition also runs on the world’s clock, not yours. Messages go unanswered for three weeks. The right role appears in month five. If coaching ends in eight weeks, it ends before the world has replied — and you are alone for the part that was actually difficult.

So the eight sessions are spread across six to eight months. Long enough for something to fail and for you to recover from it while still supported. Long enough for a new way of speaking about yourself to stop being a technique and start being simply how you speak. It is the same eight sessions and the same fee — what changes is that you are not doing the hard part alone.

The arc

What the eight sessions do

1

Ground

Where you actually are, said out loud, without managing anyone's feelings about it. We set the coaching agreement and name what must be true by session eight.

You leave with — a written statement of what this program is for.
2

Inventory

What you are carrying. Range, judgement, the capabilities that transfer across a career change or a border — including what the years out taught you that no job description lists.

You leave with — an honest inventory of what you bring, not what you lack.
3

Narrative

The story you tell about the gap, the move, the pivot. Most women in transition are apologising for the most interesting decision they ever made. We rewrite it as a decision.

You leave with — your transition told in two minutes, without apology.
4

Direction

Criteria before options. We decide what a good next chapter must contain — and what it must not — before we start judging the openings in front of you.

You leave with — a short list of non-negotiables you can hold a decision against.
5

The Ask

Visibility and reach. Who needs to know what you can do, how they will find out, and how you ask for something without shrinking the request as you make it.

You leave with — a specific list of people to reach and the words to reach them.
6

Voice

Presence in the room. Interviews, negotiations, the meeting where you say it once and it does not land. Authority is a practice, not a personality trait.

You leave with — a rehearsed way of holding the room, including the hard question.
7

Evidence

What the real world said back. We look at what actually happened between sessions — the replies, the silences, the offers — and adjust from evidence rather than from fear.

You leave with — a direction corrected by reality instead of assumption.
8

Anchor

What holds after I am gone. We consolidate what changed and hand you the questions, so the next transition does not require a coach to get through.

You leave with — your own next chapter, and the questions to run it with.

Honest framing

What coaching is, and is not

The clearer this is before we start, the more the eight sessions are worth.

Coaching is

  • A thinking partnership — you do the deciding, out loud, with someone whose only agenda is your clarity
  • Structured and confidential, held to ICF competencies and ethics
  • Forward-facing: what you do next, tested against what actually happens
  • Accountability that is warm and completely unavoidable
  • Yours — the agenda for every session is set by you

Coaching is not

  • Therapy. It does not treat trauma, depression or anxiety, and I will say so and refer you if that is what is needed
  • Consulting. I do not hand you my plan for your life
  • Mentoring. I am not here to tell you how I did it
  • A job-placement service, a guarantee of an offer, or a shortcut past the work
  • Advice dressed up in questions

Outcomes

What changes

Coaching outcomes are individual and cannot be guaranteed. These are the changes women in transition most consistently report — and what we will be measuring against your own session-one statement.

Inside

You stop apologising

The gap, the move, the change of direction becomes something you chose and can explain — rather than something you hope no one asks about.

Outward

You become findable

Your positioning, your language and your network start pointing at the same thing, so opportunities arrive rather than being hunted.

In the room

You are heard the first time

Presence, pace and the ability to hold a hard question — in interviews, negotiations and the meetings that decide things.

Decisions

You choose on criteria

You stop weighing every option against every other option, because you know what a good answer has to contain before you look.

Energy

Both lives fit

Work and family stop competing for the same hour by default. Boundaries become structural rather than a matter of daily willpower.

After

You keep the method

You leave with the questions, not the dependency. The next transition is something you can run yourself.

Bloom Forward

Coaching that pays itself forward

The women who most need this work are usually the ones least able to pay for it — because being between things is exactly what makes it expensive. So the program funds itself, in a circle.

One

You receive

You are coached through your transition — at the band you can carry, or free of charge on a Pledge or Sponsored seat if you cannot carry any of it right now.

Two

You get strong

The point of the eight sessions is that you land: a role, a business, a direction, an income. Not someday. That is what we are working toward.

Three

You pass it on

When you are steady again, you fund a seat for a woman who is where you were. She is coached, she lands, and she funds the next one.

The Pledge is a promise, not a debt. If you take a free seat, you are invited — not required — to fund a woman later, when and if you are able. It is not a loan, it carries no legal obligation, no interest and no deadline, and nothing about your coaching depends on it. It is held in honour, which is the only way it works.

Before you write

Questions worth asking

I do not know what I want yet. Is it too early?
No — that is the ordinary starting point, and sessions two to four are built for it. What would make it too early is wanting someone to tell you the answer. Coaching will not do that, and you would be disappointed.
Is this therapy?
No. Coaching works forward from where you are; it does not treat trauma, depression or anxiety. Transitions do surface grief and fear, and we can work with that as it appears — but if what you need is therapy, I will say so plainly and help you find it. The two also work well side by side.
Do you guarantee I will find a job?
No, and I would distrust anyone who did. I am not a recruiter and I do not place candidates. What the program builds is the clarity, the positioning and the presence that make a good outcome much more likely — and the ability to keep going when it takes longer than you hoped.
Which band should I choose?
The one that is true. Nobody audits it. The scale exists so that the woman who most needs the work is not the one priced out of it, and it only holds if the women who can pay more, do.
What if I cannot pay even the Access band?
Write to me anyway. Sponsored seats are funded by other women in the circle, and Pledge seats are held for women in the middle of a hard crossing. Both are free, both are the same program, and neither is a lesser version of it.
Is the Pledge legally binding?
No. It is not a loan, not a contract and not a debt. There is no interest, no deadline and no consequence if life does not allow it. Nothing about your coaching — before, during or after — depends on whether you fund a seat later.
What language, and what time zone?
Sessions are held in English or Spanish over Cal Video — a link arrives with your confirmation, and there is nothing to install and no account to create. I am based in Asunción, Paraguay, and hold slots that work for both the Americas and European afternoons and evenings.
What if I need to pause or reschedule?
Rescheduling is normal and free with reasonable notice. If life interrupts, the program pauses; the eight sessions do not expire. This work is designed for women whose weeks do not always cooperate.
How do I pay, and in what currency?
Prices are in US dollars. After our conversation you receive an invoice. If you are in Paraguay, a local transfer in guaraníes at the day’s rate is simplest and cheapest for both of us. From anywhere else, payment is by international transfer, and I confirm the exact details when you enrol. Instalments are agreed at the start, and there is no surcharge for paying in parts.
What if I want to stop partway through?
You can stop at any point. Sessions you have not used are refunded in full; sessions already held are not. No notice period, no fee, and no explanation required. If you are paying in instalments, the remaining instalments simply stop.
Do you record our sessions?
Not by default, and never without your written agreement. I am working toward the ICF PCC credential, which requires a small number of recorded sessions to be assessed. If I would like to use one of ours, I will ask you in advance and you are completely free to say no — it changes nothing about your coaching. ICF is explicit that recording without written permission breaches its Code of Ethics, and I hold to that.
Is what I say confidential?
Yes. Sessions are confidential under the ICF Code of Ethics, with the narrow legal exceptions any professional is bound by. Session hours are logged with the International Coaching Federation for credentialing — that record contains your name and the hours, never the content. Anything used publicly is anonymised and only with your written permission.
